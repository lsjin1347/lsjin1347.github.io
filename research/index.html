01

Investigation and Development of Air Pollution Management Systems

Air pollution caused by Criteria Air Pollutants (CAPs) and Hazardous Air Pollutants (HAPs) is assessed to analyze pollution trends and identify major emission sources. Based on these assessments, optimized air pollution management strategies are proposed for specific regions and pollutants.

1-1 Monitoring Air Pollution Trends Using Instrumental Analysis, Meteorological Modeling, Spatial Analysis, and Statistical Methods

  • Data from air quality monitoring stations provided by the Ministry of Environment and local governments are used.
  • Air pollutant samples are collected using particulate samplers, high-volume air samplers, and passive air samplers. The samples are chemically pretreated and analyzed using advanced analytical instruments (e.g., GC/MS, ICP-OES, and ICP-MS).
  • Statistical software, including R, SPSS, SigmaPlot, and Origin, is used to analyze air pollutant concentration data and identify pollution characteristics.
  • Geographic Information Systems (GIS) are used for spatial analysis and to create maps showing the spatial distributions of pollutants.
  • Meteorological modeling (e.g., HYSPLIT, CALMET, and WRF), receptor modeling (e.g., PMF, PSCF, and CWT), and dispersion modeling (e.g., AERMOD and CALPUFF) are used to identify major emission sources.

1-2 Assessment of Long-Range Transport of Air Pollutants Using Satellite Data, Reanalysis Data, and Meteorological Modeling

  • Satellite data from sources such as the Ministry of Environment and NASA are analyzed to assess air pollution in East Asia, including information on wildfires, Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD), and pollutant concentrations.
  • NOAA’s HYSPLIT model is used to evaluate the long-range transport of particulate matter.
  • Three-dimensional meteorological fields are simulated using meteorological models to predict the transport pathways of air pollutants.

1-3 Prediction of Air Pollutant Concentrations and Evaluation of Mitigation Strategies Using Machine Learning

  • Machine learning techniques are applied to predict air pollutant concentrations.
  • Historical data are used to estimate expected concentrations, which can be compared with real-time observations to evaluate the effectiveness of air pollution reduction policies.
  • Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) is used to identify factors contributing to air pollutant concentrations and to assess key environmental drivers.
  • Temporal trends in air pollution can be predicted for past and future periods using ARIMA/ARIMAX and LSTM-based models.
02

Health Risk Assessment of Hazardous Air Pollutants

Health risks associated with HAPs can be assessed by calculating cancer and non-cancer risks using substance-specific inhalation unit risk (IUR) and reference concentration (RfC) values. Lifetime cancer and non-cancer risks are estimated using probabilistic Monte Carlo simulations with Korean-specific exposure factors to reflect population-relevant exposure conditions over a lifetime.

2-1 Analysis of HAPs and Risk Assessment

  • HAPs, including PAHs, POPs, and heavy metals in particulate matter, are analyzed to estimate inhalation exposure levels and cancer risks.
  • Cancer risks are probabilistically estimated using exposure factors and Monte Carlo simulations to derive risk distributions for different age groups.
  • The Comprehensive Air Risk Index (CARI) has been developed to provide an integrated air quality analysis based on cancer risk assessments, facilitating a comprehensive assessment of the health impacts of air pollution.
03

Traffic-Related Air Pollution

Vehicle emissions are a major contributor to urban air pollution. The first high-resolution spatiotemporal vehicle emission inventory in Korea was developed. Estimated vehicle emissions can be coupled with vehicle-oriented air dispersion models, including RLINE and CALPUFF, and a newly developed reduced-form air quality dispersion (RFM) model to produce concentration maps of traffic-related air pollutants.

3-1 High-Resolution Spatiotemporal Vehicle Emissions

  • Vehicle emission factors in the Korean national emission inventory (CAPSS) are updated using the recent U.S. EPA MOVES5 model.
  • A bottom-up approach is used to estimate emissions at the road-link level using the most recent traffic activity data.
  • Vehicle emissions are estimated by vehicle class, hour, and road link, and the results are designed for direct application to air pollution control policies.

3-2 Reduced-Form Air Quality Dispersion (RFM) Model

  • The RFM model enables air dispersion calculations for entire urban road networks, including thousands of road links or more, using standard desktop-level computing resources.
  • A Korea-optimized RFM model is being developed with model configurations tailored to Korean meteorological and emission conditions.
04

Chemical Accident Simulation and Prioritization of Management Areas

In industrial cities, the large quantities of hazardous chemicals stored and emitted increase the potential for chemical accidents. To address this issue, chemical accident simulations using air dispersion modeling and monitoring are conducted to identify priority management areas. A Monte Carlo-based approach is applied to identify areas with high accident risks for proactive management and prevention. In the event of a chemical accident, the system predicts the real-time dispersion pathways of hazardous chemicals and provides critical information during the early stages of emergency response.

4-1 Simulation of Chemical Accidents and Prioritization of Management Areas

  • Past chemical accidents are simulated to evaluate and optimize the performance of air dispersion models.
  • Risk radii are determined by considering modeled concentrations, health risk assessments, and toxic concentration criteria.
  • Priority management areas for chemical accidents are identified by integrating modeling results, monitoring data, and emission inventories.
